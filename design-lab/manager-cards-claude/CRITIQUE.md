# Critique, refinement and comparison

The written record behind the gallery's **Critique**, **Refined** and **vs Codex** tabs.
The gallery shows the same content with the cards beside it.

## How the ten were scored

Four independent critics scored all ten directions from rendered evidence, not from the
designers' descriptions: the full card in light, dark and Arabic, all five tiers, the
silhouette test, tokens from 80 to 24px, leaderboard rows, the mini identity and the share
image. Each critic sat in a different seat:

- a brand and identity director;
- a 16-year-old from Casablanca who plays EA FC and goes to the derby;
- a 38-year-old Fantasy veteran who reads numbers carefully;
- a lead game-UI and collectibles designer.

Each scored 13 criteria from 1 to 10 and compared the ten against each other
(5 = competent but forgettable, 7 = clearly good, 9 = rare). The table is the average of
the four. A judge then named the superlatives, chose the top three and wrote a refinement
brief for each.

**Read the scores as a structured design argument, not audience research.** The critics
are Claude agents playing these four seats and scoring Claude's own concepts. Labels such as
"most likely to appeal to teenagers" are their judgement, not a finding from real
teenagers. The tests with Moroccan users that would settle them are listed at the end.

## One-line verdicts

- **01 Lucarne.** Readable at every size and owned by no one, but it is a goal pictogram with a number and a texture-only ladder. Keep its "top corner" line and ball-in-the-corner beat for the winner.
- **02 Tir.** Cut. Comet-and-swoosh sports clip art that turns into a sneaker blob at 28px, with a colour-swap ladder.
- **03 Porte-clés.** The most adult, most collectible object, with the best founder device. Needed football in the charms and the outline.
- **04 Pochoir.** The best numeral in the pool on the wrong object: a brass bronze-silver-gold ladder breaks the gold rule. Harvest the cut-through 84.
- **05 Semelle.** The bravest idea that works and the benchmark tier ladder. Needed the photo-window avatar, the brass-on-black founder wheel and the sole-showing risk fixed.
- **06 Brassard.** The right symbol on a failed object: it reads as a wristband and its five tiers look alike.
- **07 Écharpe.** Best overall. Needed craft work (knit glyphs, a woven label, distinct middle tiers, a full-size LEGEND), not a rethink.
- **08 Lsaq.** Real youth energy, but not ownable and no football in it. Reuse it as the winner's sticker export.
- **09 Panneau.** The best small-size engineering, but an 84 on a substitution board reads as player 84 or minute 84, and it sits near Codex's Broadcast.
- **10 Quatre Ombres.** The most original thought, failing as rendered: the X reads as "close" and the tiers are invisible. Keep the four-shadow light as a reveal motif.

## What the critique found across the set

- **The critics agreed.** Écharpe and Semelle were in every critic's top two; Tir and
  Brassard were at the bottom for all four. When a brand director, a teenager, a numbers
  reader and a rules reader agree this closely, it is evidence and not taste.
- **Choose the object first.** The strongest ideas (four shadows, the captain's armband,
  the stencil numeral, the top corner) sat on the weakest objects. The winners are things
  people already own and care about: the scarf, the boots, the keys.
- **Colour-swap ladders do not create status.** Only Semelle, Écharpe and Porte-clés change
  the outline at LEGEND.
- **Honest numbers are cheap to fix and expensive to ship wrong.** Brassard set values
  against the wrong labels; Panneau's dot-matrix face read 82 as B2. Every refined card was
  checked from rasterised output.
- **Meaning risks need their own gate:** a shown sole is an insult in Morocco, the board
  signals a substitution, an X means cancel, a teardrop key reads as a hotel key, and a hood
  cropped into a circle reads as an arched doorway. That last one was fixed once, in the
  shared avatar.

## The top three

**Écharpe, Semelle and Porte-clés.** The first two are the top totals. For the third slot
the judge swapped, against the totals: **Porte-clés (79.5) in, Panneau (82.8) out.**
Panneau's lead comes almost entirely from football connection and mobile scalability, but
its weaknesses are in what the object means, and refinement cannot remove them: an 84 on a
fourth official's board reads as "player 84 coming off" or "minute 84", three of four
critics raised that unprompted, and it sits close to Codex's Broadcast. Porte-clés's
weaknesses were craft and framing, which a refinement can fix. Panneau's 24px token and its
raise-the-board share are worth carrying into the winners as parts.

## The refinements

Each first pass stays in the gallery beside its refined version. Each refinement followed the
judge's brief, then a fresh reviewer compared the two versions and a last round fixed what
it found.

### 07 Écharpe → v2

- **Knit type.** Every glyph is now drawn from a stitch chart based on Changa 800, with open
  counters; all five sample names knit in full at every tier, in both scripts.
- **Hierarchy.** 84, then ALI ·26, then a narrow tier strip; one stripe per gameweek played
  over the season.
- **Founder.** ALI ·26 knitted into the name band; the cream cast-on with 2026 stays as the
  craft mark; a cast-on stripe of at least 3px survives at 24px.
- **Stats.** A woven jacquard patch with the unmodified logo and the ID along its edge.
- **Tiers.** HOMA in chunky relief, then two-colour jacquard, a finer gauge, double-knit, and
  LEGEND as a raised scarf held over the head.
- **Small sizes.** One token width per size at every tier; a fixed 64px slot in rows.
- **Remaining risk:** the raised-scarf LEGEND reads as an arch at the smallest sizes.

### 05 Semelle → v2

- **Silhouette.** A real right-foot soleplate, hung toe-up like a boot on a rack (this also
  answers the sole-showing risk).
- **Pitch geometry.** The halfway line is the flex groove, the centre circle rings the 84 and
  the penalty box is the stat plate.
- **Founder.** The brass wheel is gone: the founder sign is now a moulded year clock in a steel
  ring, with no gold anywhere.
- **Avatar.** The photo window and centred bust are gone; the hooded figure is low relief in the
  plate's material.
- **Small sizes.** A new toe-cap mini for 24–28px with the tier's stud signature.

### 03 Porte-clés → v2

- **Silhouette.** The teardrop (which read as a hotel key or a bulb) is now a dressing-room
  locker key tag, its bottom edge cut with the penalty-area D.
- **Football on the ring.** A club disc, an enamel manager pin and one season ball per season
  played.
- **Materials.** Slate aluminium, cream enamel, smoked resin and machined steel, then
  hand-stitched club-colour leather at LEGEND. There is no brass or gold. Label contrast was
  read from pixels: at least 4.7:1 on every tier.
- **Founder.** The squared split ring, the only square ring the system ever issues, with
  FOUNDER on its top bar.

## Against the Codex exploration (draft PR #377)

Three fresh judges compared each Codex top-three card with the Claude card it competes
with, from renders of both branches (Codex's cards were captured from its own branch, in
`review/codex/`): a brand director, a 16-year-old from Casablanca with his 22-year-old
cousin, and a mobile product designer who plays Fantasy. A head of design then combined
their verdicts. **Caution:** the judges are Claude agents judging Claude's work against
another tool's, so this is an argued opinion, not a neutral panel. Testing with Moroccan
users is what settles it.

| Codex pick              | Claude rival            | Verdict                                 | Vote   |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ------ |
| #1 Stadium Architecture | 07 Écharpe (refined)    | **Replace**                             | 3 of 3 |
| #2 Touchline            | 05 Semelle (refined)    | **Replace, only after a cultural test** | 2 of 3 |
| #3 Stadium Pass         | 03 Porte-clés (refined) | **Replace**                             | 3 of 3 |

- **Stadium Architecture → Écharpe.** Codex's best-finished card, but its one idea is the
  arch the brief lists as a cliché (a headstone at 28px), and it makes a state stadium the
  top reward, which young Moroccans now read through the GenZ 212 slogan "the stadiums are
  ready, but where is the hospital?". Écharpe builds prestige from the stands, with the
  founder year knitted the supporter way (ALI ·26). It still owes a LEGEND that is not
  itself an arch, middle tiers that are more than recolours, and a better 24px token.
- **Touchline → Semelle, conditionally.** Touchline is the only Codex shape you know
  without the logo, but its spine is FUT's left column, its blue is not the logo blue, long
  names overflow, it has no Arabic face and its badge drops the 84. Semelle has the most
  original outline and the best tier ladder of the twenty. The product judge voted no:
  showing a sole is an insult in Morocco, and this card puts the user's own name, often
  Mohammed or a name containing God's name, on a sole. That must be tested with Moroccan
  teenagers and parents before any more work on it.
- **Stadium Pass → Porte-clés.** A pass is issued to you, not earned. With a mandatory Fan
  ID planned for Botola matches, a laminated badge reading "MANAGER ACCESS" now looks like
  official paperwork, which breaks the not-affiliated rule. Porte-clés is owned, has an
  honest stat table and the best founder mark of all twenty (a squared ring only 2026
  founders get). It still owes tier cues before LEGEND and a 24px token that reads as a
  key tag rather than a capsule.

### On Codex's ranking

Codex ranked finish and legibility, not ownership. Stadium Architecture is its
best-finished card and Stadium Pass its most legible, and its flat drawings would be
cheaper to build and lighter at 24px than the knit, carbon and enamel renders here. But
the top of the order is wrong: the number one is the arch the brief warned against, and
the number three is the riskiest metaphor now that a Fan ID is coming (that news may have
postdated Codex's research). All three judges independently named **Touchline** as
Codex's best. Underneath, Codex's ten are one template in ten frames: the same big 84 with a
tiny OVR label, front-facing bust, four-number stat row, flag + MOROCCO + crest and dotted
founder stamp, with materials named but not drawn, no tier states, no Arabic card face and a
person-icon token at every size. I agree with the three replacements, and would run
Codex's ten as Touchline, Street, then Broadcast (as the share and head-to-head layer
rather than a card), with Terrace and Stadium Architecture after them.

Fairness cuts both ways: Claude's winners repeat a motif of their own, the hooded figure
under a dome (Écharpe's LEGEND, Semelle's midsole) and round-topped bodies. The arch
criticised in Codex's number one is partly present in this top three, and the next pass
should remove it.

## Final top five, across both explorations

This is a separate panel from the critique above: three new judges each ranked five of the
twenty, with Claude's top three in their refined versions, so the order can differ from the
critique table (which scored the ten first passes). Points were 5 for a first place down to
1 for a fifth. As with the critique, the judges are Claude agents, and four of the five
places went to Claude's own concepts.

1. **07 Écharpe (Claude, refined).** First for all three judges. The only concept in either
   set that builds Moroccan supporter culture into the object without a cliché.
2. **03 Porte-clés (Claude, refined).** The most adult object, owned rather than issued, with
   the best founder mark of the twenty.
3. **01 Lucarne (Claude).** Moved above Semelle because it carries no condition: unmistakably
   football, legible to 24px, nothing betting-like, official or political.
4. **05 Semelle (Claude, refined), provisional.** The best tier ladder and the strongest pull

5. **Touchline (Codex), reworked.** Codex's one real silhouette, kept only with rework: no
   FUT spine stack, the exact logo blue, the 84 and tier in its badge, long names, an Arabic
   face and the founder year.

### Fixed after the judges reported

Two faults the judges found were fixed before publishing: Semelle's Arabic name lost its two
dots under the tier tag (علي read as على, "on"), and Écharpe's knitted Arabic-side 26 read
as "2b". Both were re-checked in every tier with all five sample names.

### The next pass, if the owner continues

- Test Semelle's sole and the sacred-name question with Moroccan teenagers and parents first.
- Remove the hood-under-a-dome motif from Écharpe's LEGEND and Semelle's midsole.
- Give every winner's middle tiers an added part, not only a new colour.
- Redraw the 24px tokens of Écharpe and Porte-clés so they read as a scarf and a key tag.
- Show each winner in two or three real club colourways (the sample club is a deliberately
  neutral placeholder).
- Embroider the figures on Écharpe's stats patch. Its border is now stitched, but the numbers
  are still a regular typeface, so the patch can read as a printed label.
- Carry the parts the losers donated: Panneau's raise-the-board share, Lsaq's sticker
  export, Lucarne's top-corner moment, and Quatre Ombres' four-shadow light as a reveal.
